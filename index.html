On chintz

A word that went from priceless to insult, and is owed better.

Chintzy now means cheap, stingy, a little tatty. It did not start there. The history of the word is the reason this studio carries it.

1600s · the word

Chīṁṭ: spotted, bright

The name comes from the Hindi chīṁṭ, spotted or variegated, by way of the Sanskrit chitra, bright and many coloured. English borrowed the plural, chints, mistook it for a singular, and by the late eighteenth century was spelling it chintz.

1600 to 1800 · the craft

Months of work for one length

On the Coromandel Coast the cloth was drawn by hand with a kalam pen, painted with mordants and wax resists, then dyed in a fixed order and washed between every bath. Madder, indigo and chay root gave colours that would not fade. A single panel could take months.

1686 and 1720 · banned

Too good to be allowed

European mills could not match it, so their governments outlawed it. France barred chintz imports in 1686. In 1720 Parliament forbade its use in apparel or "in or about any Bed, Chair, Cushion or other Household furniture." The Court of Versailles sat outside the law and wore it anyway.

1851 · cheapened

How chintzy was born

Roller printing put the pattern on everything. Once it was everywhere it was worth nothing, and the word turned: by the middle of the nineteenth century chintzy meant shabby and mean. The pattern never got worse. Only the making did.

1980s · the flounce

Loved, then blamed

Chintz came back in country house English decorating, covered every ruffle and skirted table in sight, and took the blame when taste turned to grey minimalism. Guilt by association. The cloth was never the problem.

Now · the challenge

Back to the older meaning

The work here takes the word at its origin: bright, many coloured, drawn by hand, made slowly and meant to last. Chintz Creative is a small argument that the name is worth reclaiming, one room, one painting and one wedding at a time.
