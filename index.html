Questions

Custom watercolor and banner FAQ

How do I order a custom watercolor painting?

Fill out the commission intake form at chintzcreative.com/commission, or send a DM on Instagram to @powerspartyofsix. Include the occasion, who it is for, the details to paint, the size and your deadline. Bailey replies with a quote and timing before any painting starts.

What can be included in a custom watercolor?

Anything that tells the person's story: names, initials or a monogram crest, a birth year, a hometown, a school or team, faith symbols, pets, favorite places and objects. Each piece is designed around the details you send.

Do you make hand painted birthday banners?

Yes. Chintz Creative paints custom birthday banners by hand with the name, age and party colors, for first birthdays through milestone 30th, 40th and 50th birthdays.

Do you offer hand lettering for weddings and events?

Yes. Hand lettering is available for wedding welcome signs, menus, seating charts, place cards, framed quotes, verses and gift pieces.

How much does a custom watercolor cost?

Custom watercolors start at $50 for a letter size (8.5 × 11 in) piece with five items, such as a year, a hometown, a mascot or a favorite flower. Each extra item is $5, up to two more. Larger pieces are $100 for 11 × 14, $150 for 16 × 20 and $200 for 18 × 24. Framing in wood, gold or black with a white mat is $25 to $75 depending on size. The keepsake board shows an estimate as you lay out your piece, and Bailey confirms the price before anything is charged.

Why is the studio called Chintz Creative?

Chintz was originally hand painted cotton from India's Coromandel Coast, so prized that France banned it in 1686 and England in 1720. The name reclaims that older meaning: bright, many colored and made by hand.

On chintz

A word that went from priceless to insult, and is owed better.

Chintzy now means cheap, stingy, a little tatty. It did not start there. The history of the word is the reason this studio carries it.

1600s · the word

Chīṁṭ: spotted, bright

The name comes from the Hindi chīṁṭ, spotted or variegated, by way of the Sanskrit chitra, bright and many coloured. English borrowed the plural, chints, mistook it for a singular, and by the late eighteenth century was spelling it chintz.

1600 to 1800 · the craft

Months of work for one length

On the Coromandel Coast the cloth was drawn by hand with a kalam pen, painted with mordants and wax resists, then dyed in a fixed order and washed between every bath. Madder, indigo and chay root gave colours that would not fade.

1686 and 1720 · banned

Too good to be allowed

European mills could not match it, so their governments outlawed it. France barred chintz imports in 1686. In 1720 Parliament forbade its use in apparel or "in or about any Bed, Chair, Cushion or other Household furniture."

Now · the challenge

Back to the older meaning

The work here takes the word at its origin: bright, many coloured, drawn by hand, made slowly and meant to last. Chintz Creative is a small argument that the name is worth reclaiming, one painting at a time.
