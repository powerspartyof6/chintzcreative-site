On the word

chīṁṭ

Spotted, speckled, bright

From the Hindi chīṁṭ — spotted or variegated — by way of the Sanskrit chitra, bright. It named the painted and mordant-dyed cottons of the Coromandel Coast, drawn by hand with a kalam pen, flower by flower.

1686 · 1720

Outlawed for being too good

European mills could not match it. France banned chintz imports in 1686; in 1720 Parliament forbade its use in apparel or "in or about any Bed, Chair, Cushion or other Household furniture." Versailles wore it anyway.

1851 —

And then, cheap

Roller printing made it ordinary, and by the middle of the nineteenth century "chintzy" had become an insult. The pattern never got worse. Only the making did. This studio takes the older meaning.

About

Bailey works across rooms, paper and celebrations, and treats them as one practice.

A wedding needs the same eye as a sitting room: what the colour does at four in the afternoon, how much pattern a space can hold, where the hand shows. She paints the thing before she builds it, which is why so much of the work begins in watercolor.

Commissions are taken a few at a time so each one gets the attention it needs.
